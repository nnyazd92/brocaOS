# L-ToEC Technical Supplement: Dark Matter Drift and the Hubble Tension

## 1. The Drift Equation
We postulate that the Dark Matter to Baryon ratio $\mathcal{R}$ is not a constant, but a function of the interface scale factor $a$:
$$\mathcal{R}(a) = 5 \cdot (1 + \epsilon \cdot \ln(1/a))$$
In terms of redshift $z = 1/a - 1$:
$$\mathcal{R}(z) = 5 \cdot (1 + \epsilon \cdot \ln(1+z))$$

## 2. Cosmological Implications
### 2.1 The CMB Power Spectrum
At the epoch of recombination ($z \approx 1100$), the ratio is:
$$\mathcal{R}_{CMB} \approx 5 \cdot (1 + 7\epsilon)$$
If $\epsilon = 0.005$, then $\mathcal{R}_{CMB} \approx 5.175$.
This represents a 3.5% increase in Dark Matter density relative to baryons compared to the local universe ($z=0$).

### 2.2 The Hubble Tension
The "Hubble Tension" refers to the discrepancy between $H_0$ measured from the CMB ($\sim 67$ km/s/Mpc) and local distance ladders ($\sim 73$ km/s/Mpc).
A higher DM ratio in the early universe increases the energy density $\rho(a)$, leading to a faster expansion rate at high $z$. This alters the sound horizon $r_s$:
$$r_s = \int_0^{t_{rec}} \frac{c_s dt}{a(t)}$$
Since $a(t)$ depends on the total density, a drift in $\mathcal{R}$ provides a natural mechanism to resolve the tension without invoking "New Physics" particles, but rather a "New Physics" mapping property.

## 3. Falsification Strategy
1. **High-z Galaxy Clusters:** Measure the DM/Baryon ratio in clusters at $z > 2$. L-ToEC predicts a measurable increase compared to local clusters.
2. **CMB Sensitivity:** Re-analyze Planck data with a varying $\Omega_{DM}/\Omega_b$ model. If $\epsilon \neq 0$ provides a better fit to the high-multipole peaks, L-ToEC is supported.
